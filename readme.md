# Array Optimization: Optimizing a Circle Antenna Array Based on an Improved Social Group Optimization Algorithm [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: Circle Array (CAA) antenna arrays are superior to linear arrays due to their inherent beam steering and phase control characteristics. Antenna arrays serve as solutions for high directionality applications in wireless communications (Yin et al., 2017; Singh and Shrivas, 2017; Singh and Kamal, 2011; Dib and Sharaqa, 2012; Goldberg, 1989; Oppert, 1994; Rattan et al., 2009; Zare and Baghaiee, 2015; Pal et al., 2010; Mahto and Chobey, 2016). They can produce the desired pattern entirely by controlling the current excitation amplitude and phase along the element spacing. The characteristic feature of these antenna arrays is the current excitation amplitude and phase along the element spacing. These three parameters are the most commonly manipulated parameters of an antenna array and are determined using several complex numerical techniques to produce the required radiation pattern (Singh and Shrivas, 2017). Antenna arrays exist in one-dimensional, two-dimensional, and three-dimensional configurations. Linear arrays belong to the one-dimensional configuration and are considered the simplest form because all elements in the array are arranged on a straight line (Singh and Kamal, 2011). Similarly, in the two-dimensional configuration, we have planar arrays where all elements are distributed in two-dimensional space. Based on the above arrangement, planar antenna arrays can be circular, elliptical, rectangular, or any other shape.
Additionally, in the 3D category, elements of the array are distributed throughout the entire volume of three-dimensional space in a specific arrangement (Mahto and Choubey 2016; Al-Kubati 2009). Each configuration has its own advantages and radiation characteristics. Moreover, corresponding design strategies also vary with the configuration (Grewal et al., 2017). In this paper, circular arrays are considered due to their significant advantages (Singh and Shrivas 2017; Basu and Mahanti 2012; Li et al. 2019; Wang et al. 2017; Xin et al. 2018). In CAA, beams can be controlled through the entire circular space from 0° to 360°. Unlike the conventional array parameters such as the amplitude (I) and phase (h) of current excitation, another design parameter is called the element spacing on the circumference, which is also considered as the arc distance (d), which can be controlled according to the angle (θ) at the center of the circle. The perimeter of the circle and the distance between two consecutive elements in the array can also be controlled by changing the radius of the circle (Yin et al., 2017; Guney and Durmus, 2016; Vedula et al., 2015; Guo et al., 2018; Khodier, 2020). Therefore, there are multiple methods for controlling the element spacing appropriately.
Several evolutionary computing tools have been used for the design of circular antenna arrays. Genetic algorithms (Goldberg 1989; Oppelt 1994), particle swarm optimization, and simulated annealing (Ryngal et al. 2009) have been widely used to solve complex problems in circular antenna array design. However, these three algorithms are considered benchmarks and are often used to compare the performance of any new algorithm. Several new algorithms have been proposed and applied to CAA synthesis. For example, in recent literature, ant colony optimization (Zare and Baghaiee, 2015), weed invasion optimization (Pal et al., 2010; Mahto and Choubey, 2016), and center force optimization (Al-Kubati, 2009) have demonstrated their CAA design.
A novel social group optimization algorithm (SGOA) (Naik et al. 2018; Chakravarthy et al. 2018; Chakravarthy and Chowthy 2019; Naik and Satapathy 2020; Das et al. 2020; Verma et al. 2020; Stephen et al. 2020) and a recently proposed improved SGOA (Naik et al., 2020) were proposed for synthesizing unit CAA under constrained BW conditions with SLL optimization as the objective and compared with uniform and bird flock search (Khodier 2020) algorithms. Therefore, the organization of this paper is structured to accommodate discussions on problem statement, algorithms, and their implementations in sections 2, 3, and 4. Section 5 provides results and discussions, and section 6 concludes the paper.
The conclusion applies the MSGOA and SGOA algorithms to the comprehensive problem of CAA. For a 10-element CAA, an in-depth analysis of the three technologies' performance was conducted. The radiation pattern report shows very low SLL, with its amplitude distribution being uniform and the radiation pattern of elements spaced apart by several times smaller than that of the element spacing. It is worth mentioning that the spacing between elements has a significant impact on the array's radiation characteristics. From an optimization perspective, the clear advantage of adding additional parameters to search parameters is evident. Through the analysis of various technologies' performance, it can be seen that in circular arrays, the effect of arc length on radiation parameters is greater than that of current excitation distribution. When optimizing the arc length of CAA, the perimeter is directly affected, resulting in a considerable increase in the perimeter, which necessitates more area during the installation or realization of the antenna array. This is certainly not a disadvantage unless there are spatial limitations. The algorithm's robustness is apparent, while the corresponding BW is considered a constraint, thus maintaining a constant value of 55.60. In constrained and limited environments, the performance of both algorithms was compared with the Cuckoo Search algorithm, showing that their performance is comparable. Replacing isotropic elements with practical antenna elements and optimizing them will be the best scope for future work. Additionally, simulations can be extended to other lengths of CAA to create benchmarks.
